## 2. What kind of site this is

This is **not a job-seeker's portfolio and not a visual designer's gallery**. It is a **consultative personal site for a senior technologist**. The primary action is "book a call", which is a sales conversation, not an application. Every page therefore has one job: give a business decision-maker enough confidence in Jagdish's judgement to spend thirty minutes with him.

That changes what "portfolio" means here. The evidence is not screenshots. It is **decisions, architecture, and method**. The projects should read like short engineering case notes: the business problem, the constraint, the architectural call, what runs in production, and where humans stay in the loop.

## 3. Primary audience **[Proposal]**

Round 1 answer was "anyone". A site that addresses everyone persuades no one, so this proposal names a primary audience and treats the rest as secondary. The owner should confirm or reorder.

| Priority | Audience | What they need to see | Why they book a call |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary** | Owners and decision-makers at small and mid-sized businesses (founders, CEOs, operations and finance heads) in trade, real estate, finance, logistics, and similar operational industries, often in the Gulf, UK, US, and India | That AI can be put inside the ERP, CRM, and documents they already run, safely, by someone who has done it before and who talks ROI before technology | They have a concrete process pain and want one accountable person, not a vendor hand-off chain |
| Secondary | Partner agencies (design, marketing, product) needing a technical execution partner | Delivery capacity, engineering standards, a leader who manages scope and clients directly | They have a client and no backend or AI team |
| Tertiary | Companies seeking a fractional or full CTO | Team leadership scale, strategy ownership, cross-industry range | They need senior technical leadership without a full-time hire |

**Why this order:** the CV's "What I bring to your project" section is written to a client, not an employer. The seven-step method, the ROI language, and "book a call" all point at the primary audience. The partner-delivery line in the CV supports the secondary audience explicitly.

## 5. Core message visitors must leave with

1. **AI in production, not demos.** Document extraction, RAG, agents, and forecasting are live inside a working enterprise ERP.
2. **One accountable person.** Discovery, architecture, delivery, and support do not change hands.
3. **Business first.** Use cases are ranked by feasibility, data readiness, value, and ROI before anything is built.
4. **Safe by design.** Human review of AI output, access control, data privacy, and an architecture the client's own team can own.
5. **Proven at scale.** Teams of 25 to 50 led; eight industries delivered.

## 6. Differentiators (what the site must make visible)

- The **combination** is rare: hands-on AI architecture plus the ability to run a 25 to 50 person engineering team plus direct client ownership of scope and budget. Most AI consultants have one of the three.
- **Human-in-the-loop as a principle**, repeated across every project (ERP validation step, trade confirmation, DevOps approval). This is a trust signal and a recognisable signature. The site should make it a visible motif, not a footnote.
- **A named method** (seven steps, discovery to support). It gives the site a natural structural device and gives the visitor a sense of what working with him is like.
- **Industry range** across operational, document-heavy businesses, which is exactly where document extraction and RAG pay off.

## 7. Tone of voice **[Proposal]**

The CV already has a good voice. The site should keep it.

- **Plain and direct.** Short sentences. "AI that runs in production, not only in prototypes." No hype vocabulary (revolutionary, cutting-edge, unlock, supercharge).
- **Senior and calm.** Confidence shown through specifics, not adjectives.
- **Business-literate.** Talks about ROI, data readiness, and risk as fluently as about RAG and MCP.
- **Honest about limits.** Says where humans stay in the loop and what is still in development.
- **First person.** "I" for Jagdish's own judgement; "my team" and "we" for delivery.

Words to avoid: cutting-edge, next-gen, revolutionary, seamless, leverage, synergy, 10x, unlock, supercharge, AI-powered (as a prefix on everything).

## 8. Personality cues for the design brief

The design must express: **precision, accountability, calm authority, engineering craft**. It should feel like the website of a person who writes an SRS before writing code.

It must not feel like: a startup landing page, an AI-tooling vendor, a hackathon project, or a recruiter-facing CV with a dark theme and neon accents. The "AI consultant" genre is the single most template-prone genre on the web right now (glowing gradients, chat bubbles, robot icons, particle fields). Avoiding that look is the main creative risk and will drive the three directions in Phase 3.
