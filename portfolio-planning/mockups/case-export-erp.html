Case 01 · Export and trade · In production

An ERP where AI reads the paperwork

An export-focused ERP covering sales orders, customers, export documentation, invoicing, inventory, shipments, finance and reporting. I led the architecture and the engineering team, and designed how AI fits into daily export operations without taking decisions away from people.

2Problem

Export operations run on documents: invoices, packing lists, purchase orders, transport documents and bills of lading. Each was keyed by hand into the ERP, slowly and with errors that surfaced at the worst moment, at customs or at payment.

3Decisions

  • Extract into structured fields, then validate, then let a person approve. Nothing reaches the ledger on the model's say-so.
  • Retrieval over company documents and ERP knowledge, so staff can ask questions in plain language instead of searching folders.
  • Agents for defined workflow steps, not open-ended automation.
  • Reporting, analysis and demand forecasting assist decisions rather than make them.

Owner to supply: options considered and rejected, and why (for example fully automatic commit, a separate AI tool outside the ERP, single-vendor lock-in).

4 · Architecture

Documents enter from email, scans and uploads. The extraction step produces structured fields and a summary. Validation rules check totals, references and dates. A person reviews the result and approves it. Only then does the ERP commit the order, invoice or shipment. A second path indexes ERP data and company documents for plain-language questions. A third lets agents carry out defined workflow steps.

5Where humans stay in the loop

Reviewed before commit

Extracted data is committed only after a person approves it. Validation rules catch what they can; a person catches the rest. Forecasts inform decisions; they do not make them. The review step is a feature of the design, not a fallback.

6What runs in production

  • Document extraction and generation for invoices, packing lists, purchase orders, transport documents and bills of lading.
  • Structured field extraction, with document outputs and summaries generated from the extracted data.
  • Validation rules and a human review step before extracted data is committed.
  • Retrieval over company documents and ERP knowledge, so staff can ask questions in plain language.
  • Agents that carry out ERP workflow steps.
  • AI-assisted reporting, data analysis and demand forecasting.
